Saunas are everywhere right now, and so are the claims. Detox, longevity, fat loss, immunity. Some of it is backed by solid research. A lot of it is not.

Here is what we actually know, minus the hype.

## The big Finnish study

Most of the headlines trace back to one long-running study from eastern Finland, led by cardiologist Jari Laukkanen. Researchers followed more than 2,300 middle-aged men for about twenty years and compared how often they used the sauna with what happened to their health.

The results were striking:

- Men who used the sauna **2–3 times a week** were about **27% less likely** to die from cardiovascular causes than men who went once a week.
- Men who went **4–7 times a week** were about **50% less likely** to die from cardiovascular causes, and about **40% less likely** to die from any cause.
- A later analysis of the same group found that the most frequent users were about **66% less likely** to be diagnosed with dementia.

<Callout>
  **The fine print:** this is an observational study. It shows that frequent sauna users were healthier, not that the sauna made them healthier. People who can sauna four times a week may also have more time, less stress or healthier habits. The study also only included Finnish men, and Finnish saunas run hot (around 80°C) with sessions of roughly 20 minutes.
</Callout>

Even so, the pattern has held up in other research, and there are plausible reasons for it.

## What heat does to your body

When you sit in a hot sauna, your body responds a lot like it does to moderate exercise:

- **Your heart rate goes up** and your blood vessels widen to move heat to your skin.
- **Your blood pressure tends to drop** afterwards, and regular use is linked to lower resting blood pressure.
- **Your blood vessels get more flexible.** Researchers point to better endothelial function (how well the lining of your blood vessels works) as one likely reason for the heart findings.
- **Inflammation markers tend to be lower** in regular sauna users.

That is why some researchers describe the sauna as "passive cardio." It is not a replacement for exercise, but it seems to train some of the same systems.

## Mood and sleep

Many people say they sleep better after a sauna, and this fits with how the body cools down after heat. The research on mood is earlier-stage but promising. Small studies of whole-body heat treatment have found improvements in symptoms of depression. Nobody should swap treatment for a sauna, but the calm after a good session is not just in your head.

## What the sauna does not do

- **Detox.** Your liver and kidneys handle that. Sweat is mostly water and salt.
- **Burn fat.** The weight you lose in a session is water, and it comes back as soon as you drink.
- **Cure a cold.** The evidence for immune benefits is thin.

## The benefit nobody measures

The Finnish men in that study were not sitting alone in silence. In Finland the sauna is a social place: family, friends and neighbours sit together, talk, and go quiet together.

We cannot prove it, but we suspect that matters. Strong social connection is one of the most consistent predictors of a long, healthy life in all of public health research. A weekly hour of heat, cold and conversation with the same group of people might be doing more than the heat alone.

<PullQuote>
  Maybe the secret was never just the heat. Maybe it was who you sat next to.
</PullQuote>

## Using the sauna safely

- **Drink water** before and after.
- **Start with short rounds** of 8–12 minutes and leave earlier if you feel dizzy.
- **Skip the alcohol.** It is the most common factor in sauna accidents.
- **Talk to your doctor first** if you have heart problems, low or uncontrolled blood pressure, or are pregnant.
